# Sabah Basin Tectonic History

## Overview
The Sabah Basin is located in northern Sabah, East Malaysia, at the collision zone between the Eurasian Plate and the Philippine Sea Plate. The basin evolved through five major tectonic phases from the Late Eocene to the present.

**CRITICAL UPDATE (NSPW synthesis 2026-07-16):** The basin is NOT a simple passive-margin fold belt. It is dominated by the North Sabah–Pagasa Wedge (NSPW), a >500 km syn-collisional, mobile-shale-dominated, sediment-loaded wedge formed after Dangerous Grounds continental crust underthrust NW Borneo. The NSPW is the mechanical spine connecting deep tectonic forcing to shallow stratigraphy and petroleum systems.

## Phase 1: Syn-Rift / Platform Setup (Paleogene–Early Miocene, 40–23 Ma) — ABKSS: ASAS + BEBAS
- PSCS rifting; Dangerous Grounds rift blocks
- Deep marine turbiditic deposition of the Panas Thicknessnam Formation
- Nido Limestone growing on Dangerous Grounds platform
- Source rock potential: TOC 0.5–3.0%, Type II kerogen
- BEBAS (Oligocene intra-rift reorganisation) = Arif's independent contribution

## Phase 2: Collision Onset (~23 Ma) — ABKSS: KAPUR — BMU/TCU
- Dangerous Grounds continental crust enters subduction zone
- Cannot subduct (buoyant) → underthrusts NW Borneo
- End of Crocker accretionary prism era
- Beginning of NSPW as post-collisional syn-orogenic wedge
- **This is NOT the DRU.** BMU/TCU marks collision onset; DRU marks wedge-top.

## Phase 3: Wedge Growth (23–13 Ma) — NSPW Active Phase
- Massive sediment flux from Borneo highlands → NW Sabah margin
- NSPW builds to 8–9 km thick pile
- Imbrication, folding, mass transport, sediment incorporation
- Overpressures develop in Miocene shales
- Mini-basins / pocket basins form (2–15 km wide, 3–4 km deep)
- Shale expulsion begins

## Phase 4: Slab Breakoff + Shale Expulsion (~13–10.5 Ma) — ABKSS: SABAR — DRU
- PSCS slab breaks off (Pilia et al. 2023 tomography)
- Buoyant rebound uplifts NW Borneo hinterland (Crocker Range: 24–60 km crust)
- DRU carved into shelf — NOT collision onset, it is wedge-top
- Massive delta systems begin prograding
- Shale-expulsion basins develop above overpressured mobile shale
- **DRU reclassification:** from "collision unconformity" to "wedge-top / slab breakoff response"

## Phase 5: Mud Canopy + Post-Wedge Burial (10.5–0 Ma) — ABKSS: SENJA
- Overpressured shales expelled through pipes and vents
- 50 mud volcano centres coalesce into 1900 km² canopy
- Late middle-to-early late Miocene turbidites deposited around canopies
- Rotan field turbidite sandstones deposited in this window
- SRU (~8.5 Ma): post-canopy burial by prograding shelf/delta systems
- Late gravity-driven DWFTB develops
- Present-day mud volcanoes still active

## Key Unconformities (NSPW-Reclassified)
- BMU/TCU (23 Ma) — collision onset / NSPW base
- DRU (13 Ma) — wedge-top / slab breakoff response (NOT collision start)
- UIU (10.5 Ma) — end of mini-basin development
- SRU (8.5 Ma) — post-canopy burial
