# Sabah Basin Tectonic History

## Overview
The Sabah Basin is located in northern Sabah, East Malaysia, at the collision zone between the Eurasian Plate and the Philippine Sea Plate. The basin evolved through four major tectonic phases from the Late Eocene to the present.

## Phase 1: Rift + Ophiolite Emplacement (Late Eocene–Oligocene, 40–28 Ma)
- Rifting and ophiolite obduction created the basement
- Deep marine turbiditic deposition of the Panas Thicknessnam Formation
- Source rock potential: TOC 0.5–3.0%, Type II kerogen

## Phase 2: Passive Margin + Delta (Late Oligocene–Early Miocene, 28–16 Ma)
- Post-rift thermal subsidence
- Deltaic progradation (Kellaneous Formation)
- Carbonate platform development (Gomantong Limestone)
- Best reservoir quality in Oligocene section

## Phase 3: Collision + Inversion (Middle–Late Miocene, 16–12 Ma)
- Arc–continent collision
- Structural inversion creating anticlinal traps
- Marine transgression (Labang Shale — regional seal)
- Turbidite deposition (Labang Sandstone)

## Phase 4: Wrench Reactivation (Pliocene–Recent, 12–0 Ma)
- Strike-slip reactivation
- Delta progradation from the north (Sandakan Formation)
- Primary reservoir target for offshore Sabah

## Key Unconformities
- MIO_UNC (12 Ma) — end of carbonate platform
- BASE_LABANG (16 Ma) — top of carbonate / base of seal
- BASE_OLIGOCENE (28 Ma) — post-rift onset
