# Dangerous Grounds — the impactor province

This is not a basin and not a chart column: it is the CRUSTAL BLOCK that
drifted north Palawan/South China conjugate margin into Borneo and thereby
built the three-position system. Layang-Layang Basin is its platform/pinnacle
realm; the Sabah Trough is its front margin bent downward.

## History compressed
1. Eocene rifting off the South China conjugate margin (R1; R2 37–32 Ma
   separated by the patchy IRU — Chang et al. 2023).
2. Breakup in the later Early Oligocene, diachronous — BU is a process surface,
   and its age varies with position; on highs, carbonates start before BU.
3. Thermal sag + Late Oligocene–Early Miocene carbonates on highs and on the
   flexural forebulge shallow zone (Steuer et al. 2014).
4. Southward transport on the SCS plate while its floor spread (C12 onset
   ~33–32 Ma — IODP Exp 349).
5. COLLISION_MM (debated ~20.5 vs 16.9–15.5 Ma): the block jams the NW Borneo
   trench; SCS spreading dies (Clift et al. 2008; Hinz 1985 Middle-Miocene
   unconformity).
6. Post-collision: the block goes DOWN and starves (drowning, pelagic drape),
   while its load makes the neighbour go UP — same cause, opposite sign.

## The underthrust continuation (the missed geometry)
The stretched crust does not stop at the collision front: at least halfway
between the Sabah shelf and the slope toe it continues SE beneath the Neogene
sediments of Borneo. A plan-view basin map hides this; at depth part of
Kinabalu is STACKED on top of Layang-Layang crust. Status INTERPRET — the
admissibility test is a well-tied dip line across the trough.

## What remains unknown
- Complete syn-rift package: never penetrated; lithology "highly uncertain".
- OCT zonation / true oceanic extent beneath the province.
- Whether the carbonate record drowns in steps (Chen 2023) or reefs largely
  kept up (Hutchison 2010).
