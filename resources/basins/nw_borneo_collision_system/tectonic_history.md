# NW Borneo Collision System — cross-section logic (NW → SE)

```
NW                                                              SE
 Layang-Layang        Sabah Trough           Kinabalu Basin      Borneo
 (lower plate)        (foredeep)             (upper plate)       (Crocker)
 carbonate + drape    distal fill      /\/\ thrust front / mini-basins / uplift
 ~~~~ RU ~~~~~        ~~~~~~~~~~~     ///  DRU unconformity  ///
 syn-rift grabens  ── bends down ──>  Dangerous Grounds crust continues underneath
 ─── ROU / BU ───                      (underthrust beneath the wedge)
```

## The one-sentence paradox
These are not three separate basins: they are three positions within ONE
collision system. Layang-Layang sits on the lower plate (continental crust
rifted off the South China margin, moved south). The Sabah Trough is the
flexural moat where that lower plate bends down under the load. The Kinabalu
Basin sits on the upper plate — basin fill resting on the Borneo accretionary
wedge. The same tectonic event therefore leaves a different stratigraphic
signature in each area, and no single Stage chart can correlate them literally.

## The part usually missed (EVIDENCE relay + INTERPRET)
The stretched, rifted crust of the Dangerous Grounds continues SE beneath the
Neogene sediments of Borneo, at least halfway between the Sabah shelf and the
toe of the slope. So at depth, Layang-Layang crust underlies part of Kinabalu:
Kinabalu is not simply next to Layang-Layang — part of it is STACKED on top of
it. A plan-view basin map hides this. Status: INTERPRET, not confirmed —
admissible only once a dip line with a well tie crosses the trough (see
standing_holds).

## Second paradox — sediment source runs opposite
- Kinabalu: sediment from uplifted Borneo (sand-rich deltas + turbidites). Reservoirs abundant; charge and trap are the main risks.
- Layang-Layang: sediment-starved; carbonate + pelagic drape. Reservoir tied to carbonate highs; charge from a syn-rift source is the main question.
- Trough: distal leftovers only.

## Third paradox — naming
"Sabah Trough" = bathymetric and flexural feature, not a basin with its own
origin. "Layang-Layang Basin" = a rifted-margin province. "Kinabalu Basin" =
a group of wedge-top and foreland depocentres. The three names mix a
physiographic feature, a crustal block, and a set of depocentres.

## Tests (falsifiable)
1. One enterprise dip line from Layang platform, across trough, into Kinabalu
   wedge — physically tests the RU/DRU relationship instead of naming it.
2. Backstripping: post-COLLISION_MM residual subsidence curves of trough vs
   Kinabalu should diverge with opposite sign.
3. Zircon U-Pb provenance flip across the collision (north-derived vs
   Borneo-derived sand) dates the loading independently of NN zones.
