Status: Draft v1

Confidence: MED-HIGH

## Executive Verdict

**CLAIM:** Layang-Layang / Dangerous Grounds and Kinabalu Basin are not separate Earth stories. They are different expressions of the same Sabah regional tectonic machine.

**INTERPRETATION:** Layang-Layang is the stronger rift-drift / carbonate-high archive, while Kinabalu is the stronger compression / loading / toe-thrust / sediment-routing archive.

**HYPOTHESIS:** The most useful signal is not the absolute age of each horizon alone, but the change in thickness, stacking, onlap, truncation, and deformation across equivalent tectono-stratigraphic boundaries.

## 1) Horizon-by-Horizon Contrast Table

| Horizon / boundary | Layang-Layang / Dangerous Grounds signal | Kinabalu Basin signal | Regional meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-rift basement / acoustic basement | Pre-existing crustal fabric and inherited highs/lows beneath later rifting. | Basement is more often masked by later shortening, uplift, and loading. | Establishes inherited template before the margin diverges. |
| ROU / earliest rift onset | Onset of extension, fault-block creation, half-graben development. | Not the dominant visible signal; later deformation often overprints earlier rift geometry. | Marks the start of accommodation creation on the NW Sabah margin. |
| Syn-rift package / IRU | Thickening into grabens and half-grabens; syn-sedimentary faulting is a key control. | Local syn-rift inheritance may exist, but the stronger record is later tectonic inversion and deepwater loading. | Thickness contrast here records where the crust was stretching and storing sediment. |
| BU / breakup unconformity | Major phase boundary: end of main rift faulting and transition into post-rift development. | Equivalent regional surface is often read through later deformation and sediment routing rather than preserved carbonate growth. | A regional tectono-stratigraphic hinge. |
| Early Oligocene to Early Miocene carbonate interval | Carbonate platform growth on structural highs is a key Layang-Layang signal. | Carbonate is secondary to clastic deepwater / shelf-slope systems and deformation. | Highs and low sediment flux favor carbonate preservation; loading and clastic supply suppress it. |
| RU / later unconformity surfaces | Collision/loading-related deformation, drowning, burial, or erosion over the earlier carbonate-rift package. | Major regional shortening, toe-thrusting, and fold-thrust development become clearer. | Records transition from rift-drift to compressional basin response. |
| Miocene clastic interval | Post-rift siliciclastic drape and deepwater sedimentation increase. | Strong clastic progradation, slope deposition, and deepwater fairway development. | Sediment routing becomes the dominant control. |
| Toe-thrust / fold-thrust structures | Present, but not the defining primary archive of Layang-Layang. | Dominant structural expression in Kinabalu / deepwater NW Sabah. | Compression and gravity tectonics are the strongest late-stage signal in Kinabalu. |
| Present-day geometry | Platform-to-trough transition with carbonate highs and deepwater depocentres. | Hinge-zone, fold-thrust, and deepwater-to-shelf structural complexity. | Same margin, different tectonic expression. |

## 2) GEOX-Style Literature Review Note

### Claim 1

**CLAIM:** Dangerous Grounds / Layang-Layang preserve a rift-to-drift sequence with syn-rift grabens, breakup surfaces, and post-rift carbonate growth on structural highs.

**evidence_for:**
- Banerjee & Salim (2021) describe deep-water Dangerous Grounds stratigraphy in the NW Sabah Platform region and present a sequence-based interpretation of the area.
- Das et al. (2024) identify rift onset, intra-rift, breakup, and later unconformities within the strati-structural evolution of NW Sabah / Dangerous Ground.
- Hutchison (2004) frames the southern South China Sea margin as a passive margin / underthrusting system with the Dangerous Grounds as a key structural element.
- The GEOX internal Layang-Layang notes describe rotated fault blocks, half-grabens, listric faults, and a carbonate interval tied to the breakup window.

**evidence_against:**
- Some of the available public evidence is abstract-level or snippet-level rather than full-text in this session.
- Later compression and collision overprint can blur the pure rift signal in the final preserved geometry.

**missing_tests:**
- Full text correlation of the Oligocene carbonate top/base against picked horizons.
- Independent well-to-seismic tie across the exact Layang-Layang line used internally.
- Thickness maps segmented by boundary package.

**uncertainty:** PLAUSIBLE → CLAIM at basin scale; PARTIAL on horizon-by-horizon ages until the full line picks are tabulated.

### Claim 2

**CLAIM:** Kinabalu Basin is better read as a compressional, accretion-related, and deepwater loading system than as a simple rift-carbonate basin.

**evidence_for:**
- Tjia (1988) explicitly frames Kinabalu in terms of suture and accreted terrane architecture.
- Balaguru & Hall (2008) describe Sabah tectonic evolution as a product of multiple regional episodes, including NW-SE compression in the Late Eocene, Early Miocene, and Middle Miocene.
- Khamis et al. (2018) describe deepwater Sabah toe-thrust trends and link them to structural kinematics and sedimentary patterns.
- Franke et al. (2008) interpret NW Sabah/Borneo as a collision zone with an elongated imbricate deepwater fan and toe-thrust architecture.

**evidence_against:**
- Kinabalu also inherits earlier basin geometry and cannot be reduced to compression alone.
- The exact boundary between inherited extensional fabric and later inversion is location-dependent.

**missing_tests:**
- Correlation of local horizons against the regional Sabah unconformity stack.
- Structural restoration across the hinge-zone and toe-thrust domain.
- Better separation of inherited basement geometry from Miocene compression.

**uncertainty:** CLAIM for the structural style; HYPOTHESIS for the exact local partitioning of inherited vs younger deformation without line-by-line restoration.

### Claim 3

**CLAIM:** The most meaningful contrast between Layang-Layang and Kinabalu is thickness and stacking across equivalent boundaries, not simply basin names or absolute age.

**evidence_for:**
- The contrast primitive in GEOX already treats observation minus expectation as the discovery signal.
- The NW Sabah review literature repeatedly ties unconformities and structural phases to basin evolution.
- The Deepwater Sabah toe-thrust literature shows sedimentary fairways and structural style are coupled.

**evidence_against:**
- If boundary picks are inconsistent, thickness contrast can become an artifact of interpretation rather than a tectonic signal.
- Without a stable horizon nomenclature, the same label can hide different physical surfaces.

**missing_tests:**
- Single horizon lexicon across Layang-Layang and Kinabalu.
- Common time-depth / age framework.
- Cross-line thickness normalization and uncertainty propagation.

**uncertainty:** HYPOTHESIS.

## 3) Regional Tectonic Synthesis

**CLAIM:** Sabah is a connected tectonic system involving South China Sea opening, Dangerous Grounds evolution, collision / underthrusting, NW Sabah shortening, and later gravity-driven deepwater deformation.

**evidence_for:**
- Balaguru & Hall (2008) identify repeated NW-SE compression linked to proto-South China Sea subduction and major tectonic episodes in Sabah.
- Tongkul (1991) and related Sabah tectonic studies describe the development of elongate basins, compression, and later deformation in Sabah.
- Hutchison (2004) and the later Dangerous Grounds papers place the southern South China Sea margin into a linked rift-drift / collision framework.
- The toe-thrust and deepwater fold-thrust literature shows younger compressional and gravity-tectonic systems overprinting the older margin architecture.

**evidence_against:**
- Regional models differ in emphasis: some stress subduction / accretion, others gravity tectonics, others margin collision.
- Local basin interpretation can overfit one model if the entire margin is treated as uniform.

**missing_tests:**
- A unified line-by-line correlation from Dangerous Grounds through Sabah Trough into the Kinabalu / deepwater fold-thrust domain.
- Explicit mapping of which horizons are truly equivalent surfaces versus only approximate sequence boundaries.

**uncertainty:** PLAUSIBLE.

## 4) GEOX Summary

**INTERPRETATION:**
- Layang-Layang reads like the margin that remembers extension, breakup, and carbonate high growth.
- Kinabalu reads like the margin that remembers accretion, loading, toe-thrusting, and deepwater sediment redistribution.
- The contrast is meaningful because the same regional tectonic engine changes expression with crustal position, sediment supply, and structural inheritance.

**HYPOTHESIS:** If the same boundary package thickens in Layang-Layang but folds or truncates in Kinabalu, that contrast is probably telling you where the system shifted from accommodation creation to deformation and loading.

**UNKNOWN:** The exact horizon correspondence still needs a dedicated correlation table with line IDs, pick ages, and uncertainty bands.
