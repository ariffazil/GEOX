## 4. The 8 Eureka Insights (Source: Huang et al. 2021)

### EUREKA 1 — Ductile Mid-Crustal Layer is REAL and IMAGEABLE
- ~5 km thick mid-crustal ductile layer at ~8–13 km depth in Zhongsha Trough
- Separated from lower crust by ~0.3 km/s velocity step (PcP phases, ~90 km traceable)
- **ARIF-relevant:** Layang-Layang sits on same Dangerous Grounds microplate — ductile layer almost certainly exists under Layang-Layang too.

### EUREKA 2 — Detached Upper Crust is Regional Architecture
- ~20 km wide dismantled upper-crust window in Zhongsha Trough
- Rotated basement blocks on both flanks as rollover structures
- Seaward-dipping detachment fault soles into ductile layer at ~7 km below basement
- Repeats on OBS2011-1 (SW Dangerous Grounds) and OBS2014s (Liyuexi Trough)
- **ARIF-relevant:** This is the kinematic template for Layang-Layang Basin. The synthesis already mentioned thin sediment cover over volcanic basement (Pekaka-1, Tepat-2) and >100 nT magnetic anomalies. The match is direct.

### EUREKA 3 — Hyperthinned Continental Crust at OCT (~6 km thick)
- ~6 km thick crust with stretching factor β ≈ 5
- Two-layer velocity structure resembling oceanic crust
- Underlain by partially serpentinized upper mantle (Vp ~7.7 km/s, ~13% serpentinization)
- Strong magnetic anomaly (~170 nT) = prerupture magmatic intrusion / volcanic extrusion
- **ARIF-relevant:** The COB you've been mapping is NOT a sharp line. It is a ~50 km wide transition zone of hyperthinned continental crust + intrusions + serpentinized mantle.

### EUREKA 4 — Velocity is the Crust-Type Decoder Ring

| Zone | Vp signature | Thickness | Diagnostic |

| Normal continental (Xisha Bank) | 5.0–6.8 km/s gradual | ~22 km | Receiver function match |

### EUREKA 5 — Magmatism Heats Up the Crust → Triggers Ductile Decoupling
- Franke et al. (2014) supported: prerupture magmatism heats the crust, reduces viscosity, enables interlayer decoupling
- Thickened lower crust under Zhongsha Trough = magmatic underplating/intrusion compensating Moho uplift (like Kenya and Baikal rifts)
- **ARIF-relevant:** Layang-Layang volcanic basement is **not just "syn-rift volcanics"** — it is the **rheological enabler** of the entire basin architecture. Magma is not decoration. It is the mechanism.

### EUREKA 6 — Breakup Propagated East→West, Stalled by Indochina
- 32–23 Ma: breakup propagated east to west at much lower rate than spreading
- Stalled by far-field compression from Indochinese block (Le Pourhiet et al. 2018; Jourdon et al. 2020)
- ~1000 km wide western margin
- Rift structures striking in propagation direction: Xisha Trough, Zhongshanan Basin = E-W; Zhongsha Trough, Liyuexi Trough = NE-SW after 15° rotation
- **ARIF-relevant:** Layang-Layang Basin is the southwestern continuation of this same propagator system. The orientation of Layang-Layang sub-basins and the obliquity of structures across Sabah margin are the southern expression of the SCS breakup propagator stalling against Borneo.

### EUREKA 7 — Serpentinized Mantle Lives Under Hyperthinned Crust
- Below ~6 km thin crust at Zhongshanan Basin, Vp drops to ~7.7 km/s over >3 km depth
- ~13% partial serpentinization generated by water penetrating through high-angle normal faults that cut into upper mantle
- Heat flow ~60 mW/m² → too cold for partial melting → must be serpentinization, not melt
- **ARIF-relevant:** If refraction data across Layang-Layang → NW Sabah Trough shows sub-Moho velocity drop to ~7.7, **you have direct evidence of serpentinite**. Massive implications for heat flow and hydrocarbon kitchens.

### EUREKA 8 — Ductile Layer Geometry Predicts Where Magma Stops
- Top of lower crust shows prominent velocity/density contrast enhanced by a front of intruded sills blocked at the ductile boundary
- Magma rising through lower crust gets trapped at brittle-ductile interface and intrudes laterally as sills
- **ARIF-relevant:** Many "mysterious" mid-crustal bright reflectors and sill-like geometries seen in Sabah deep seismic **may not be sedimentary**. They may be sills frozen at the ancient ductile boundary. Wholly new lens for re-interpreting deep seismic over Layang-Layang.
